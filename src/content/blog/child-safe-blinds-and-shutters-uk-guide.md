_Last reviewed: October 2026._

If you have young children, grandchildren who visit, or a baby on the way, window coverings deserve more thought than colour and fabric. Most blinds and shutters are perfectly safe — but **looped cords and chains** are a genuine hazard for small children, and they are easy to overlook in a room you see every day.

This guide covers what we recommend on surveys across [Leicester](/locations/shutters-in-leicester/), [Loughborough](/locations/shutters-in-loughborough/) and Leicestershire: the cord-free options, what the UK rules actually say, how to get a nursery dark enough to sleep, and what to do about older blinds already in the house.

## The Short Answer

- **The hazard is cords and chains**, not blinds or shutters as such.
- **[Plantation shutters](/our-shutters/) have no cords at all** — they are the simplest cord-free choice for bedrooms and family rooms.
- **[Motorised blinds](/blind-motorisation/)** run from a remote, app or switch, so nothing hangs down.
- **Wand-operated blinds** swap cords for a rigid rod — a lower-cost cord-free option on some ranges.
- **Corded blinds must have safety devices fitted correctly** — that is the installer's job, not an optional extra.
- **For nurseries, safety and darkness are separate questions** — shutters solve the first; a blackout layer often solves the second.

## Why Cords and Chains Are the Risk

Young children are curious, climb on furniture and do not understand that a loop of cord can tighten. A long chain beside a cot, bed or sofa is within reach in a way adults rarely notice, because we are looking at the window, not at what hangs below it.

That is why the advice from the safety bodies is consistent: **remove the loop, or secure it out of reach**. Everything else in this guide is a way of doing one or the other.

## What the UK Rules Say

Since 2014, internal blinds sold in the UK must meet the child-safety requirements in **BS EN 13120**. In practice that means a new blind is either:

- **Safe by design** — no accessible looped cord or chain in the first place, or
- **Supplied with child-safety devices** — cleats, chain tensioners or breakaway connectors that keep cords secured or stop a loop forming.

Crucially, **the installer is responsible for fitting those devices** where they are needed. A blind delivered in a box with a safety device still in the packet does not meet the intent of the rules.

The [British Blind and Shutter Association](https://www.bbsa.org.uk/) runs the **Make It Safe** campaign to spread the same message. Shutter Envy is a [BBSA Accredited Specialist](/bbsa-accredited-specialist-shutter-envy/), and we follow that guidance on every survey.

## Cord-Free Options, From Simplest to Most Flexible

### 1. Plantation shutters

[Plantation shutters](/our-shutters/) are hinged panels fitted into a frame inside the window reveal. You tilt the louvres by hand and open the panels like small doors. There are **no cords, chains or loops** anywhere.

They also suit busy family homes for other reasons: they wipe clean, there is no fabric to pull down, and they last for many years. For most children's rooms we recommend [faux wood shutters](/faux-wood-shutters/) — durable, moisture-resistant and good value.

![White plantation shutters in a bedroom — top louvres open for daylight, lower louvres closed for privacy, no cords or chains](/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/627260925_1256657506526519_4783057165808453020_n.jpg)

Split tilt or a [tier-on-tier layout](/choosing-the-perfect-style-full-height-vs-tier-on-tier-shutters/) lets you close the lower louvres for privacy while the top stays open for daylight — handy in a child's room that faces the street or a neighbouring house.

### 2. Motorised blinds

[Motorised blinds](/blind-motorisation/) are raised and lowered by remote, phone app, voice assistant or schedule. With nothing hanging down, they are a strong choice for:

- **Tall windows and stairwells**, where a manual blind would need a very long chain.
- **Rooms where furniture has to sit near the window**, such as a small bedroom.
- **Families who want blackout fabric** on a roller blind without any cord.

Rechargeable battery motors mean most homes need no rewiring. Motorisation is available across most of our [blind ranges](/our-blinds/), including rollers.

### 3. Wand-operated blinds

On some blind ranges, a rigid wand replaces the cord or chain. It is a lower-cost cord-free option where motorisation is not in the budget. We will tell you on survey which ranges and sizes can be specified this way — it varies by product and window size.

### 4. Corded blinds with safety devices — fitted properly

Corded blinds are not banned, and in some rooms they remain sensible. The non-negotiable is that the safety devices are **fitted, secured and at the right height**, and that cords are not left loose or tied together. If a blind with a cord or chain is going into a child's room, we will usually suggest one of the cord-free options above instead.

## Nurseries and Children's Bedrooms: Safety and Darkness

Parents usually want two things from a nursery window: **no hazards** and **enough darkness for daytime naps**. They are different problems.

- **Safety:** shutters or motorised blinds remove cords completely.
- **Darkness:** shutters make a room much darker when closed, but light can still edge around the panels and between the louvres. If your child needs near-total darkness in a bright south-facing room, add a blackout layer or choose a dedicated blackout system.

## Are Shutters Completely Child-Proof?

Nothing in a home is completely child-proof, and we would rather be honest about it. Shutters remove the cord hazard, but a few practical points are worth knowing:

- **Hinges and closing panels** can catch small fingers, just like a cupboard door.
- **Louvres are sturdy but not climbing frames** — teach children not to hang on panels or pull louvres.
- **Windows must still open.** Shutters are hinged, so they swing clear and the window behind can open fully — important for ventilation and, on upper floors, for escape.
- **Window restrictors stay usable.** On survey we check that panels clear handles and restrictors so you never have to choose between a shutter and a safety catch.

## What to Do About Older Blinds in the House

Many families move into homes with blinds already fitted. A quick check, room by room:

1. **Look for loose loops** — any cord or chain that hangs in a loop and is not secured.
2. **Check for safety devices** — a cleat on the wall, a chain tensioner fixed at the bottom, or a breakaway connector on the cord.
3. **Check the height** — cords should be secured well out of a child's reach, not tucked behind a bedside table.
4. **Decide: secure or replace.** If the blind is in good condition, a correctly fitted safety device may be enough. If it is worn, the device is missing, or it is in a child's bedroom, replacing it with a cord-free option is usually the better answer.

When children are part of the brief, we look at:
