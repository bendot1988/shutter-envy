Deciding on the right window dressing is a significant step in any home renovation. At Shutter Envy, we often hear the same question from homeowners in [Loughborough](/locations/shutters-in-loughborough/), Leicester, and across Charnwood: ‘***Are window shutters actually worth the money***?’

While blinds and curtains have their place, [shutters](/our-shutters/) offer a structural permanence and aesthetic appeal that other treatments simply cannot match. To help you decide, we have broken down everything you need to know about costs, benefits, and the practicalities of choosing shutters for your home.

## How much do shutters cost for an average window?

Cost is naturally the first consideration. In the UK, shutters are generally priced by the square metre. For a standard-sized window, you can expect to pay **anywhere from £180 to £350** per square metre, depending on the materials and the complexity of the installation.

### How much should I budget for shutters?

Budgeting for shutters requires looking at the ‘**total project cost**‘ rather than just the price per window. For a typical three-bedroom semi-detached house in areas like [Rothley](/locations/shutters-in-rothley/) or [Quorn](/locations/shutters-in-quorn/), a full-house installation usually ranges between £2,500 and £5,000.

Several factors influence this price:

- Material: MDF (Craftwood) is the most cost-effective, while premium hardwoods or waterproof ABS (for bathrooms) sit at a higher price point.
- Window Shape: Standard rectangular windows are straightforward. Special shapes, such as arched or circular windows, require bespoke templates and craftsmanship, increasing the cost.
- Installation Type: Full-height shutters, café style (covering the bottom half), or tier-on-tier designs all vary in price.

### 1. Increased Property Value

Unlike curtains, shutters are considered a 'fixture'. They stay with the house when you sell it. Estate agents often note that high-quality shutters can increase the 'kerb appeal' and overall valuation of a property because they suggest a well-maintained, premium home.


### 2. Superior Insulation and Energy Efficiency

Shutters act as an extra layer of glazing. In the winter, they trap a layer of air between the window and the room, reducing heat loss. In the summer, they reflect sunlight away, keeping your rooms significantly cooler. Over time, this can lead to noticeable savings on your energy bills.


### 3. Durability and Longevity

A high-quality set of shutters from Shutter Envy is built to last decades. While blinds may fray or fade and curtains require expensive dry cleaning, shutters only need a quick wipe with a damp cloth to stay looking new for years.

## What are the disadvantages of shutters?

We believe in being transparent with our customers. While we love shutters, they aren’t perfect for every single scenario.

- **Initial Cost**: The primary disadvantage is the upfront investment. They are more expensive than off-the-shelf blinds or curtains.
- **Fixed Nature**: Once installed, they are a permanent part of the window. You cannot simply ‘swap them out’ if you change your mind about the colour next month.
- **Lead Times**: Because our shutters are custom-made to your exact measurements, there is a waiting period for manufacturing and delivery.

### When not to use shutters?

You might reconsider shutters if you have very narrow window sills that cannot accommodate a frame, or if you have inward-opening windows (common in some European-style builds) that would be obstructed by the shutter panels.

## Light and Privacy: Finding the Balance

A common concern is: ‘***How much light do you lose with shutters?***‘ While shutters do have a frame and louvres (slats) that take up some space, they actually offer better light control than any other option. By tilting the louvres, you can bounce light off the ceiling to brighten a room while maintaining total privacy from passers-by. If you are worried about losing light, we recommend wider louvre sizes (such as 76mm or 89mm), which allow more sunlight to flood in when open.

## Do shutters deter burglars?

Security is an underrated benefit of shutters. When closed or tilted, they act as a physical and visual barrier.

- **Visual Privacy**: They prevent opportunists from seeing high-value items inside your home.
- **Physical Barrier**: An intruder would have to break through a locked wooden or polymer structure after breaking the glass, which creates noise and takes time. This serves as an excellent secondary deterrent.
