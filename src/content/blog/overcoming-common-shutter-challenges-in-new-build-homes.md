### 1. Not Enough Clearance Around the Window

Many new homes have shallow reveals or wide sills that leave very little room once plaster and sealant are finished. Sockets, alarm sensors, or radiator pipes often sit exactly where a shutter frame needs to go.

Our solution: fully bespoke frames built to the millimetre. We design slim profiles, offset hinges, and notched panels so everything fits perfectly – even around tricky handles, tiles, or skirting.

### 2. Slightly Out-of-Square or Uneven Window Frames

New properties settle during the first 12–24 months. A window that looks square on handover can develop a tiny twist or slope. Standard shutters would leave uneven gaps or cause panels to rub.  
We fix this by:

Using tolerant frame styles that mask minor movement  
Measuring with extra clearance in settling homes  
Checking diagonals and light gaps on the day of installation

### 3. Measurement Mistakes on ‘Identical’ Windows

Developers often repeat the same window design across a street, so homeowners assume every opening is identical. In reality, plaster bulges, tile lips, or construction tolerances can vary by several millimetres.

Our surveyors always measure width at three heights, check both diagonals, and note every obstruction. That way your shutters arrive ready to fit first time – no awkward returns or delays.

### 4. Fixing into Fresh Plasterboard or Newly Painted Walls

Lightweight blocks and plasterboard are standard in new builds around Thrussington, Sileby, and Cropston. Fresh plaster can still hold moisture, and paint may not be fully cured.

We use the correct hollow-wall anchors, test every fixing point, and – when needed – delay installation by a week or two so the wall is entirely stable. The result is shutters that stay firmly in place for decades, with no sagging or cracked plaster.
