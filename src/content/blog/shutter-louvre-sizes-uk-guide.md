Once you have chosen a material and a style, there is one more decision that changes how your shutters look every single day: **louvre size**. It is the width of each horizontal blade, and it affects how much light and view you get, how the shutter suits the window, and even whether everything clears your window handles.

This guide covers what we recommend on surveys across [Leicester](/locations/shutters-in-leicester/), [Loughborough](/locations/shutters-in-loughborough/) and Leicestershire: the five common sizes, which windows suit each one, and the tilt options that go with them.

## The Short Answer

- **Louvre sizes typically run from 47mm to 114mm.** The common sizes are 47mm, 63mm, 76mm, 89mm and 114mm.
- **63mm and 76mm are the all-rounders** — they suit most casements, bays and room sizes.
- **Go smaller (47mm–63mm)** on small, cottage-style or bathroom windows.
- **Go wider (89mm–114mm)** on large windows and glazed doors where you want the view.
- **Closed, all sizes give similar privacy.** The difference shows when the louvres are open.
- **Check clearance.** Wider louvres need more depth behind the panel — we measure this on survey.

## What Louvre Size Actually Changes

### Light and view

When the louvres are tilted open, wider blades mean **fewer louvres and bigger gaps**. More daylight comes in and the view outside is less broken up. Smaller louvres give a finer, more traditional pattern with more blades across the window.

### Proportion

A louvre size that looks perfect on a tall living-room window can look heavy on a small bathroom window. As a rule, **smaller windows suit smaller louvres** and **larger windows suit wider louvres**, so the panel does not end up with only a handful of blades.

### Clearance

When louvres tilt open, they project behind the panel towards the glass. **Wider louvres project further.** On uPVC windows with protruding handles, or in shallow reveals, that can matter. We measure the depth of every reveal and adjust the frame or recommend a smaller louvre so nothing catches. If you have uPVC windows, our [guide to fitting shutters on uPVC windows](/can-you-fit-shutters-on-upvc-windows-uk-guide/) covers this in more detail.

### Cost

Wider louvres such as 89mm and 114mm typically cost **a little more** than standard 63mm. Material, number of panels and window shape have a far bigger effect — see our [plantation shutters cost guide](/how-much-do-plantation-shutters-cost-a-complete-uk-price-guide/) for 2026 ranges.

## The Five Common Sizes

### 47mm — small windows and a traditional look

The smallest common size. It gives a fine, detailed pattern that suits **small cottage windows, landings and bathrooms**, and period homes where a delicate look feels right. On larger windows it can look busy and lets in less view when open. Our [cottage window shutters guide](/cottage-window-shutters-uk-buyers-guide/) covers smaller and older windows.

### 63mm — the classic all-rounder

The size most people picture when they think of plantation shutters. 63mm works on **standard casements in terraces, semis and new builds**, on most bays, and in kitchens and bedrooms. It is the safe choice when you want shutters that look right in every room.

### 76mm — a little more contemporary

76mm sits between classic and wide. It opens up more of the view than 63mm without looking oversized, which makes it popular on **living-room windows, bays and wider casements**. It suits both period and modern homes.

### 89mm — wide windows and views

89mm louvres give a bold, open look with fewer blades. They suit **large windows, tall glazing, modern bays and glazed doors**, where you want to see out when the louvres are open. Check clearance carefully on windows with deep handles. Our [Portchester® aluminium install in Market Harborough](/security-meets-style-in-market-harborough-portchester-aluminium-shutters/) used 89mm louvres with silent tilt.

### 114mm — the widest, for large openings

The widest common size, best kept for **very large windows and doors** in contemporary rooms. It gives the most open view, but it needs enough depth and panel height to look right, and it is not available in every range or material.

## Tilt Rod, Hidden Tilt or Split Tilt?

Louvre size and tilt control are usually chosen together:

- **Centre tilt rod** — the classic vertical rod on the front of each panel. It moves all the louvres together and gives a traditional plantation look.
- **Hidden (silent) tilt** — no visible rod. You move the louvres by hand and the mechanism sits in the edge of the panel. Cleaner and more modern, and popular with 76mm and 89mm louvres.
- **Split tilt** — the top and bottom louvres move independently, usually divided by a **midrail** lined up with the window's transom. It gives privacy below and daylight above in a single full-height panel.

If you want separate top and bottom panels that open independently, that is a different style — see [full-height vs tier-on-tier shutters](/choosing-the-perfect-style-full-height-vs-tier-on-tier-shutters/) and [café-style shutters](/cafe-style-shutters/).
