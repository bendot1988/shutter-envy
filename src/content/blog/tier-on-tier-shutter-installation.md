**Tier-on-tier shutters** are a style of interior window shutter with two separate sets of panels stacked vertically. The top and bottom panels operate independently, giving you flexible control over light and privacy.

We recently fitted a pair of white tier-on-tier sets on side-by-side windows in a grey bedroom/study — a clear showcase of how this style works in a real home.

## How tier-on-tier works

Unlike full-height shutters (one panel per opening from sill to head), tier-on-tier splits each window into an upper and lower panel. You can:

- Open the **top** for a garden or street view while keeping the **bottom** closed at sofa height
- Close both tiers and still tweak louvre angle for soft daylight
- Fold the upper panels fully back when you want maximum glass

If you are still choosing a layout, our guide to [full-height vs tier-on-tier shutters](/choosing-the-perfect-style-full-height-vs-tier-on-tier-shutters/) walks through the trade-offs.

## The install

On this project, each of the two windows got its own tier-on-tier pair in crisp white, fitted to sit neatly with the period moulding and radiators below.

### Top open, bottom closed

This is the classic tier-on-tier “party trick” — upper panels folded open against the reveal, lower panels closed with louvres open for light without giving up privacy at eye level.

### Both tiers closed

With both tiers shut, the windows still look fitted and bright — louvres angled to pull daylight across the desk and carpet.
