_Last reviewed: September 2026._

Bifold doors and wide sliding patio doors are brilliant for light and garden access — and awkward to dress. Curtains bunch, vertical blinds rattle, and a standard hinged shutter would swing straight into the kitchen table. **Tracked shutters** solve that: plantation shutter panels that run on an overhead track, then fold or slide clear so you can still use the doors.

This guide covers what we recommend on surveys across [Leicester](/locations/shutters-in-leicester/), [Loughborough](/locations/shutters-in-loughborough/) and Leicestershire: bifold vs bypass tracks, realistic 2026 costs, materials, and when a door-mounted blind is the smarter choice.

## The Short Answer

- **Tracked shutters are made for wide door openings** — bifolds, sliding patio doors and large glazed runs.
- **Bifold tracks** fold panels into a stack at one or both sides — the widest clear opening.
- **Bypass tracks** slide panels behind each other — ideal for sliding patio doors and tight rooms.
- **Stack space is the big planning point** — folded panels need somewhere to live when open.
- **[Faux wood](/faux-wood-shutters/)** suits most kitchen-diners; **[aluminium](/aluminium-plantation-shutters/)** for harsh sun, humidity or security on the rear elevation.
- **Expect roughly £900–£3,500+ fitted** depending on width, panel count and material.
- **Blinds can be the better call** on very wide or constantly used doors — we will say so on survey.

## Why Doors Need a Tracked System

A hinged shutter on a window opens a few degrees into the room and stays out of the way. A door run is different: the panels are tall, the span is wide, and you need a clear path to the garden several times a day.

Common problems we see on Leicestershire extensions:

- **Swing clearance** — hinged panels on a 3-metre opening would hit dining chairs, islands and radiators
- **Handle projection** — door handles and locking levers need panels that sit proud of them
- **Stack width** — six to eight folded panels take up real wall space when open
- **Track fixing** — the head of the opening must take screws into something solid, not just plasterboard
- **Floor level** — tiles, thresholds and underfloor heating affect whether a bottom guide is used

That is why tracked shutters are a survey-led product. Even a small measuring error multiplies across eight panels — see why we never rely on [self-measured sizes](/why-measuring-yourself-can-be-a-costly-mistake/).

## Bifold Track Shutters

Panels are hinged together in pairs and hang from carriers on an overhead track. To open, you fold them back concertina-style so they stack neatly to one side (or split to both sides on wide runs). Once stacked, the doors behind open fully.

**Best for:** bifold doors, kitchen extensions, garden rooms and any opening where you want the full width clear.

**Watch for:** the stack. A 3.5-metre run might need 50–70cm of wall beside the opening for the folded panels — we plan that around furniture and radiators before quoting.

## Bypass Track Shutters

Bypass panels do not fold. They sit on two or more parallel tracks and slide past each other, so one panel parks behind the next. Close them all and you get a continuous shutter wall; open them and the panels stack in depth rather than width.

**Best for:** sliding patio doors, rooms with no spare wall for a folded stack, and openings where you only ever use one door leaf.

**Watch for:** you never get the whole width clear at once — some glass is always behind parked panels. On a standard sliding patio door that is rarely a problem, because only one pane opens anyway. ## Bifold vs Bypass: Which Suits Your Doors?

| Your doors | Usually best | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Bifold doors (3+ leaves)** | Bifold track | Full opening when doors fold back |
| **Sliding patio door (2 panes)** | Bypass track | Only one pane opens; no stack needed |
| **Wide run, tight side walls** | Bypass track | Panels park in depth, not width |
| **French doors** | Usually hinged, not tracked | See our [French door shutters guide](/plantation-shutters-for-french-doors-uk-buyers-guide/) |
| **Very wide, used all day** | Consider blinds | Door-mounted systems move with each leaf |

## Materials for Tracked Door Shutters

| Material | Verdict | Best used |
|---|---|---|
| **Faux wood / PVC composite** | Practical default | Kitchen-diners, steamy cooking areas, busy family rooms |
| **Hardwood** | Premium, lighter per panel | Dry living and dining rooms wanting a natural finish |
| **Aluminium plantation** | Toughest option | South-facing glass, garden rooms, rear-elevation security |
| **MDF** | Not recommended | Tall door panels and kitchen humidity punish it |

Tall door panels are heavy, so weight matters on a track. Hardwood is lighter per panel; faux wood copes best with kitchen steam — see our [kitchen shutters guide](/kitchen-shutters-uk-buyers-guide/). ## What We Check on a Tracked Shutter Survey

1. **How you use the doors** — daily garden access, or mostly closed with one leaf in use
2. **Stack position** — which side the panels park, and what furniture or radiators sit there
3. **Track fixing** — lintel, ceiling or reveal, and whether it will take the panel weight
4. **Handle and lock projection** — so panels clear every lever
5. **Floor finish** — tiles, thresholds and underfloor heating for any bottom guide
6. **Louvre size and mid-rail height** — matched across the run so it reads as one wall

## When Blinds Are the Better Choice

Tracked shutters are not automatic for every door. Consider blinds when:

- The doors are opened constantly and you do not want to fold panels first
- Side walls leave no room for a stack and bypass would cover too much glass
- Budget is tight across a very wide run
- You want blackout or insect mesh that moves with each door leaf — see [BlindScreen®](/blindscreen/) and our [best blackout blind for bifold doors](/best-blackout-blind-for-bifold-doors-leicestershire/)
