A honeycomb blind is the made-to-measure option we specify when a room needs privacy and a less chilly pane, and shutters are more than the window needs. The fabric is folded into cells. That cell is the whole point: light, privacy and temperature in one blind, with a blackout version when the room has to go dark.

This guide is for homeowners in [Leicester](/locations/shutters-in-leicester/), [Loughborough](/locations/shutters-in-loughborough/) and Leicestershire. It covers how the blind works, standard drop versus top-down bottom-up, blackout and SmartFrame, where it earns its place, and what we check before anything is ordered.

## The Short Answer

- **Honeycomb blinds suit bedrooms, cold windows, lofts and home offices.**
- **The cell holds air**, so the blind takes the edge off a cold pane in a way a flat roller does not.
- **A standard blind drops from the top.** Top-down bottom-up also covers the lower glass and leaves light above.
- **Blackout fabric stops light through the cloth.** SmartFrame is what we use for near-perfect light blocking at the edges.
- **A front bay still looks better in shutters.** Honeycomb is the blind to choose there if the shutter budget waits.
- **Plan on about £150–£300 fitted** for a standard window. See the [blinds cost guide](/how-much-do-made-to-measure-blinds-cost-uk-2026/).
- **We measure on site.** Fabric, frame and motor are chosen for that opening.

## What a Honeycomb Blind Is

[Our blinds range](/our-blinds/) includes roller, Venetian, honeycomb, duo roller and vertical. A honeycomb blind is the fabric one with a cell. You may also hear it called a cellular blind. Same product.

Each fold traps a pocket of air. Closed, the blind covers the glass for privacy and softens the light. The air in the cell is why it helps with room temperature, which a single sheet of roller fabric cannot do. We fit them in a wide range of fabrics and colours. We do not publish a heat-loss percentage, because the result depends on the glass, the fabric and how tightly the blind sits in the reveal.

## Standard Drop or Top-Down Bottom-Up

| How you want to use it | Better starting point | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Close the window fully, open it fully | Standard bottom-down | Simple, and usually the lower price |
| Privacy at sofa or bed height, light above | Top-down bottom-up | The lower glass stays covered while the top lets daylight in |
| A bedroom that must go dark | Blackout fabric, often with SmartFrame | The cloth blocks light; the frame deals with the edges |
| A tall window, a skylight, or a blind you cannot reach | Motorised honeycomb | Remote, hub or voice, with no cord to pull |

A standard honeycomb blind works like a roller: it drops from the headrail and stops where you leave it. That is enough for most bedrooms and for a window you simply want covered at night.

**Top-down bottom-up** is the versatile one. The blind can lower from the top as well as rise from the sill. On a street-facing lounge in Leicester or Loughborough, that means the lower half stays private while light still enters above the sightline. It is the honeycomb answer to what café-style shutters do on a bay, at a lower cost and in fabric rather than panels. If the bay is the front of the house and you want that look from the pavement, start with [café-style shutters](/cafe-style-shutters/) and use honeycomb where the budget or the opening points that way.

## Blackout and SmartFrame

Blackout honeycomb uses a fabric that blocks light through the material. That is not the same as a dark room. A blind that stops short of the frame still lets a line of light in at the sides, which is what wakes light sleepers.

**SmartFrame** is the option on our honeycomb range for near-perfect light blocking. It is the specification to ask for when the bedroom faces east, a street lamp sits outside, or someone works shifts. Our [bedroom blackout guide](/are-plantation-shutters-blackout-bedroom-shutter-guide/) compares that with shutters, including shutters with a blackout blind behind the frame when you want the strongest close.

A filtering fabric is the better everyday choice in a living room or a home office. You still get the cell and the privacy, and the room does not go black at four o'clock in November.

## Where Honeycomb Earns Its Place

- **Bedrooms**, when you want darkness or privacy and you are comparing blinds rather than [bedroom shutters](/bedroom-shutters-uk-buyers-guide/).
- **A cold pane**, including older single glazing and north-facing glass, where the cell is doing useful work.
- **A loft or a skylight**, where heat sits at the roofline and a cord is awkward to reach. Our [shutters versus blinds comparison](/shutters-vs-blinds-honest-uk-comparison-2026/) puts blackout honeycomb or shutters as the two sensible loft options.
- **A home office**, when you need to cut glare on a screen and still see the room.
- **A street-facing window that is not a showpiece bay**, where top-down bottom-up covers the sightline.

Wide bifold and sliding doors are a different job. The blind has to stack clear of the doors. For those openings, start with our [motorised blinds for bifold doors](/motorised-blinds-for-bifold-doors-uk-buyers-guide/) rather than treating a window honeycomb as the same product.

## When a Roller, a Duo or Shutters Fit Better

| The brief | Better starting point |
|---|---|
| Lowest cost, a simple open-and-close blind | Roller blind |
| Striped fabric that shifts from private to see-through | Duo roller |
| A front bay, a period window, or a finish you want to keep for years | Plantation shutters |
| Tilted slats and a wood or wood-effect look | Venetian blind |
| A tall landing over the stairs | Shutters, so nothing hangs on a cord |

A roller is the right blind when you want a crisp fabric and you do not need the cell or a top-down option. It is usually the lower price. A [duo roller](/our-blinds/) gives you privacy and a view by sliding two layers of fabric, which is a different trick from a honeycomb cell.

[Plantation shutters](/our-shutters/) are still what we fit on most street-facing bays. They have no fabric to replace, and they follow the shape of the window. Honeycomb is the honest alternative when you want the privacy and the warmer pane now, and shutters can wait. Plenty of Leicestershire homes use both: shutters on the front, honeycomb on a bedroom or a loft.

## What It Costs in 2026

For a standard window, a made-to-measure honeycomb blind commonly sits around **£150–£300 fitted**. That is the planning range in our [blinds cost guide](/how-much-do-made-to-measure-blinds-cost-uk-2026/), which also prices rollers, Venetians, duo rollers and motorised rollers.

Three things move a honeycomb above that band:

- **A wide or tall opening**, including a loft window or a run of panes
- **Blackout fabric with SmartFrame**, because you are paying for the edge seal as well as the cloth
- **Motorisation**, by remote, hub or voice

We quote after measuring. Two windows that look the same from the kerb often differ once the reveal, the handles and the fabric are on the survey sheet.

## What We Check on a Survey

- **Reveal depth and shape**, so the headrail and any frame sit properly
- **Which way the window opens**, so the blind clears the handle and the sash or casement
- **Condensation and which room it is**, so we specify a fabric that suits a bedroom, a kitchen or a loft
- **How dark the room needs to be**, filtering fabric or blackout with SmartFrame
- **Who can reach it**, which is what pushes a stair, a loft or a tall window towards [motorisation](/blind-motorisation/)
- **Children in the room**, because a dangling cord is the wrong default

## Cords, Children and Motors

Honeycomb blinds can be motorised with a remote, a hub or voice control. That is the specification we prefer in a child's room and anywhere the blind is out of easy reach. A motor removes the cord you would otherwise pull every morning.

If cord safety is the reason you are replacing an old blind, read the [child-safe blinds and shutters guide](/child-safe-blinds-and-shutters-uk-guide/) before you choose. Shutters avoid cords entirely. A motorised honeycomb is the blind route to the same outcome.
