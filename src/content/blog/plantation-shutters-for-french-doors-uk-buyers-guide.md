_Last reviewed: July 2026._

French doors are one of the most common “can you actually put shutters on these?” questions we hear on surveys across [Leicester](/locations/shutters-in-leicester/), [Loughborough](/locations/shutters-in-loughborough/) and the wider county. The short answer is **yes** — [plantation shutters](/our-shutters/) work well on most French door pairs, provided they are made to measure around the way the doors open.

This guide covers the options (hinged vs framed vs tracked), realistic 2026 costs, materials, and what we check before quoting.

## The Short Answer

- **Yes — shutters and French doors go together** when the layout respects handles and swing clearance.
- **Door-mounted hinged panels** are the most common solution: shutters open with each leaf.
- **A separate frame** can work where the reveal is deep enough and you want shutters independent of the door swing.
- **Tracked systems** are usually for wider patio or bifold runs — not every French pair needs a track.
- **Expect to pay more than a standard casement** — height and hardware drive the quote.
- **Bifolds are a different job** — see our [bi-fold door shutter install](/bi-folding-door-shutters-installation-in-leicestershire/) and [BlindScreen®](/blindscreen/) if that is what you have.

## How Shutters Work on French Doors

A good French-door shutter layout does three things at once:

1. Gives you louvre control for privacy and glare.
2. Lets you open the doors fully without panels clashing.
3. Clears the handle and lock furniture.

![White plantation shutters on French doors with one leaf ajar, casting striped sunlight across a tiled floor.](/images/blog/french-door-shutters-hinged-open.jpg)

On most Leicestershire semis and extensions, that means **hinged shutter panels fixed to each door leaf** (or to a slim frame on the leaf), so when you open the door the shutters travel with it. Café-style half-height panels are less common on doors because you still need clearance at handle height — full-height with carefully placed mid-rails is the usual answer.

## French Doors vs Bifolds vs Patio Doors

| Opening | Typical shutter approach | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **French doors** (hinged pair) | Hinged panels per leaf | Most common; design around handles |
| **Patio / sliding doors** | Tracked or bypass | Panels slide clear of the glass |
| **Bifold / bi-fold** | Tracked door shutters or BlindScreen | Different geometry — [see bifold projects](/bi-folding-door-shutters-installation-in-leicestershire/) |

If you are unsure which doors you have, a photo on WhatsApp before survey helps — but the tape measure still decides the final layout.

## Materials That Suit French Doors

| Material | Best when… | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| [Faux wood](/faux-wood-shutters/) | Living rooms, kitchens, busy family homes | Painted look only |
| Hardwood | Dry dining rooms wanting timber warmth | Avoid steamy rooms |
| Aluminium | Security, south-facing heat, large spans | Premium — [aluminium plantation shutters](/aluminium-plantation-shutters/) |
| MDF | Rarely our first pick on doors | Heavier; moisture risk — [MDF verdict](/are-mdf-shutters-any-good-honest-uk-verdict/) |

Ground-floor French doors onto a side return or alley sometimes justify [Portchester aluminium](/portchester-aluminium-shutters-leicester/) for security as well as light control. Most garden-facing pairs in quiet estates are perfectly happy in faux wood.

## What Does It Cost in 2026?

French doors are taller and more hardware-heavy than a bedroom casement, so they sit above a “per standard window” average.

**Planning guide (supply and fit, mid-range):**

- **Faux wood pair:** roughly **£400–£900+**
- **Hardwood pair:** often **£700–£1,400+**
- **Aluminium / security-spec:** higher — quoted after survey

Exact figures depend on door width and height, louvre size, colour, and whether mid-rails or shaped cut-outs are needed around handles. ## What We Check on a French Door Survey

![White plantation shutters on uPVC French doors with circular cut-outs around the handles — a typical survey detail.](/images/blog/french-door-shutters-privacy-closed.jpg)

Mark (or another surveyor) will look at:

1. **Handle and lock projection** — so louvres and mid-rails clear the furniture.
2. **Swing path** — furniture, radiators and curtain poles that block a full open.
3. **Reveal depth** — whether door-mount or a separate frame is cleaner.
4. **Door material** — timber, uPVC or aluminium (see also [shutters on uPVC](/can-you-fit-shutters-on-upvc-windows-uk-guide/)).
5. **Overlook and security** — rear alley vs open garden changes the material conversation.

DIY measuring is especially risky on doors — small errors show immediately when a leaf binds. See [why measuring yourself can be costly](/why-measuring-yourself-can-be-a-costly-mistake/).

## Common Myths

**“Shutters only work on windows.”**  
Doors are a core use case when designed properly.

**“I’ll never get the doors fully open.”**  
That is a specification problem, not a product limit.

**“Blinds are always cheaper and just as good.”**  
Blinds can win on budget. Shutters usually win on permanence, insulation and a fitted look — especially on a main living-room elevation.
