## The DIY Route: Pros and Cons



Advantages

- **Initial Cost Savings:** The primary draw of DIY is the removal of labour costs. You pay only for the materials and shipping.
- **Flexible Timeline:** You can work at your own pace without scheduling appointments with external contractors.
- **Personal Satisfaction:** For seasoned hobbyists, there is a sense of accomplishment in completing a home improvement project independently.

Disadvantages

- **The Risk of Measurement Errors:** Shutters require millimetre-perfect measurements. If your windows are slightly out of square—which is common in older properties in areas like Swithland or Woodhouse Eaves—standard DIY kits may not fit.
- **Lack of Specialist Tools:** Proper installation often requires specific power tools and fixings that the average homeowner may not possess.
- **No Warranty on Labour:** If you damage the shutter or your window frame during installation, you are responsible for the replacement costs.
- **Complexity:** Assembling frames and ensuring louvres operate smoothly is more technical than hanging a simple curtain pole.

## Essential Advice for Homeowners

When deciding between DIY and professional fitting, consider the ‘Window Health Check’:

- Check for Squareness: Measure your window diagonally from corner to corner. If the two measurements differ, your window is ‘out of square’, and DIY shutters will likely leave unsightly gaps.
- Material Weight: Real wood or high-quality PVC shutters are heavy. Installing these alone can be dangerous and lead to dropped panels.
- Future-Proofing: If you are interested in home automation, professional installation is the only way to guarantee that motorisation packages—such as remote-controlled roller blinds—are set up safely.
