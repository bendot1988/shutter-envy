**It’s a fair question. “Faux wood” doesn’t exactly sound like a premium product — and if you’re spending serious money on window dressings, you want to know you’re getting something that will actually last. So let’s skip the sales pitch and give you an honest answer: are faux wood shutters any good?**

The short answer is yes — genuinely. But like any product, they’re better suited to some situations than others. This guide covers everything you need to know: what they’re made of, how long they last, what they cost, and how they stack up against real wood. By the end, you’ll know exactly whether faux wood shutters are the right choice for your home.




![faux wood shutters kitchen window uk](/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/faux-wood-shutters-kitchen-window-uk-1024x571.jpeg)




## What Are Faux Wood Shutters?





Faux wood shutters are made from a high-grade PVC or a composite material that’s engineered to replicate the appearance of natural timber. The best versions are virtually indistinguishable from real wood once fitted — same clean lines, same satisfying louvre action, same painted finish. The key difference is what’s underneath: instead of timber, you have a material that doesn’t absorb moisture, doesn’t warp, and doesn’t respond to temperature changes the way wood does.

That distinction matters more than it might seem, particularly in a UK climate where humidity fluctuates and condensation on windows is a fact of life for most homeowners.





## Are Faux Wood Shutters Any Good? The Honest Verdict





**Yes — and for most UK homes, they’re actually the smarter choice over real wood. *Here’s why*:**

They’re moisture-resistant, making them suitable for kitchens, bathrooms and utility rooms where real wood can warp over time.
They’re easy to clean — a wipe down with a damp cloth is all they need, with no special treatments or polishes required.
They hold their colour well. Unlike painted timber, high-quality faux wood won’t yellow or fade with prolonged exposure to sunlight.
They’re lighter than real wood, which puts less strain on the frame and fixing points over time.
The quality gap between faux wood and real wood has narrowed significantly. Modern PVC shutters from a reputable manufacturer look excellent.
The caveat: quality varies. Budget faux wood shutters from a discount supplier can look and feel cheap, with louvres that don’t sit flush and finishes that mark easily. The material is only as good as the manufacturer behind it, which is why sourcing matters.

## How Long Do Faux Wood Shutters Last?

A well-made faux wood shutter, properly fitted and reasonably maintained, should last well over a decade — in many cases closer to 20 years. Because the material doesn’t absorb moisture, it won’t crack or split in the way timber can. Because it doesn’t expand and contract significantly with temperature changes, the louvres stay true and the panels keep their shape.

In practical terms, faux wood shutters will outlast most fabric blinds and curtains by several years, and they’ll do so without the maintenance that natural wood demands. The most common reason shutters need replacing before the end of their natural life is damage — a broken louvre rod, a hinge that’s worked loose, a panel that’s been knocked — rather than material degradation.

## Are Faux Wood Shutters Expensive?

Relative to real wood shutters, no. Faux wood is the most accessible entry point into the plantation shutter market, typically running from around £150 to £350 per window fully fitted — compared to £300–£600 for natural timber. Across a full property, that difference adds up considerably.

Relative to other window dressings, shutters of any material represent a higher upfront investment than curtains or standard blinds. But they’re not a like-for-like comparison. A good quality faux wood shutter will last many times longer than a fabric blind, requires almost no maintenance, and adds a level of finish to a room that most window dressings simply can’t match. Viewed over a 15-year period, the cost per year is often comparable to or cheaper than repeatedly replacing cheaper alternatives.





## Faux Wood vs Real Wood Shutters: Which Is Better?

This depends entirely on where the shutters are going and what you value most.

#### **Choose faux wood if:**

- **The shutters are going in a kitchen**, **bathroom**, or any room with **high humidity**
- **You want a lower price point** without sacrificing the lookEasy maintenance is a priority
- **You’re fitting a rental property** or want a practical, hard-wearing option

#### Choose real wood if:

- You want the **warmth and texture of natural timber** and are happy to pay for it
- The shutters are in a **dry living area** where moisture isn’t a concern
- You’re planning to stain rather than paint, to showcase the natural grain
- You want the **absolute premium finish**, and longevity is less of a concern than aesthetics

For the majority of UK homes, faux wood is the more practical and cost-effective choice. Real wood is the right call when budget isn’t the deciding factor and you want to make a statement.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### Are faux wood shutters waterproof?

They're highly moisture-resistant rather than fully waterproof. This makes them well-suited for kitchens and bathrooms — steam and splashes won't cause damage — but they're not designed for outdoor use or direct water contact.


### Do faux wood shutters look cheap?

Not if you buy from a reputable supplier. Poor-quality faux wood can look plasticky, but well-made PVC shutters with a good paint finish are genuinely difficult to distinguish from real wood once fitted. The key is the quality of the finish and the precision of the manufacturing.


### Can faux wood shutters be painted?

Most faux wood shutters come pre-finished in a range of colours and aren't designed to be repainted at home. Unlike timber, the surface doesn't take paint in the same way. If you want a specific colour, it's worth choosing it at the order stage rather than trying to paint them later.


### Are faux wood shutters worth it?

For most homeowners, yes. They offer the look of a premium window dressing at a more accessible price point, they're built to last, and they require very little upkeep. If you're comparing them to replacing cheap blinds every few years, the economics stack up clearly in favour of shutters over the long term.
