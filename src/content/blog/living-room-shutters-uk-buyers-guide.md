The living room is usually the first window guests see from the pavement — and the room you actually sit in at night with the lights on. **Living room shutters** have to do both jobs: look considered from the street, and give you privacy at sofa height without turning the room into a cave.

This guide covers what we recommend on surveys across [Leicester](/locations/shutters-in-leicester/), [Loughborough](/locations/shutters-in-loughborough/) and Leicestershire: full-height vs café vs [tier-on-tier](/choosing-the-perfect-style-full-height-vs-tier-on-tier-shutters/), materials that suit a dry front room, realistic 2026 costs, and when curtains or blinds are the better answer.

## The Short Answer

- **Yes — plantation shutters suit most living rooms**, especially street-facing casements and bays.
- **[Tier-on-tier](/choosing-the-perfect-style-full-height-vs-tier-on-tier-shutters/)** is the default for pavement-facing windows: privacy below, daylight above.
- **[Café-style](/cafe-style-shutters/)** keeps the top glass clear if you want a permanent garden or street view.
- **Full-height** suits rear elevations, overlooked gardens, or evening blackout-style control.
- **[Faux wood](/faux-wood-shutters/)** is the practical default in dry living rooms on uPVC; hardwood is for period joinery and a timber look.
- **Expect mid-hundreds to low thousands fitted**, depending on width and whether it is a bay — not a catalogue price from a photo.
- **We measure on site.** Handle clash, radiator shelves and shallow reveals decide the spec.

## Why Living Rooms Are Different from Kitchens and Bedrooms

A [kitchen shutter](/kitchen-shutters-uk-buyers-guide/) has to cope with steam and taps. A [bedroom shutter](/are-plantation-shutters-blackout-bedroom-shutter-guide/) is judged on darkness for sleep.

A living room is judged on three things at once:

- **Daytime privacy** from the pavement or a close neighbour, without losing sky light
- **Evening control** when lamps are on and the glass becomes a fishbowl
- **A fitted elevation** that still looks right from the street — this is the window that sells the house in photos

Fabric curtains can do the evening job. They rarely do the daytime job as cleanly as louvres you can angle at sitting height. ## Full-Height, Café or Tier-on-Tier?

### Tier-on-tier (most street-facing rooms)

Independent upper and lower panels. You close the bottom at sofa height and open the top for daylight — the layout most Victorian and 1930s front rooms actually use during the day.

![White tier-on-tier plantation shutters on a street-facing living-room window, lower panels closed for sofa-height privacy and upper panels open for daylight](/images/blog/living-room-shutters-tier-on-tier-privacy.jpg)

### Café-height

Lower shutters only; the top glass stays clear. Good when you want a permanent view of the sky or a front garden, and you do not need to darken the whole window. Common on shallower sills where a full stack of panels would feel heavy.

### Full-height

Panels from sill to head. Better on rear living rooms, overlooked side returns, or open-plan spaces where the TV wall needs glare control across the whole pane. If the window is a bay, full-height and tier-on-tier are both common — cost and panel count are covered in our [bay window shutters cost guide](/bay-window-shutters-cost-uk-2026-guide/).

## Materials for a Dry Living Room

Living rooms are usually dry, so you have more material choice than a kitchen or bathroom.

| Material | Living-room verdict | Best used |
|---|---|---|
| **Faux wood / PVC composite** | Best default on uPVC | Most Leicestershire semis and new builds |
| **Hardwood** | Premium timber look | Period sashes, painted or stained interiors |
| **MDF** | Fine in a dry lounge | Budget full-height on even reveals — avoid if the room runs damp |
| **Aluminium plantation** | Niche | Strong sun, security-led specs, or to match aluminium doors |

If the frames are white or anthracite uPVC — typical in Clarendon Park conversions and Loughborough new builds — start with our [uPVC shutters guide](/can-you-fit-shutters-on-upvc-windows-uk-guide/). Hardwood still works; it just needs a reveal deep enough for the louvres to open.

## What We Check on a Living-Room Survey

Mark (or another surveyor) measures with the room as you use it:

1. **Sightlines from the pavement and opposite windows** — this decides café vs tier-on-tier more than fashion does.
2. **Reveal depth and window handles** — louvres must clear glass and espagnolette handles ([uPVC notes](/can-you-fit-shutters-on-upvc-windows-uk-guide/)).
3. **Radiator and sill** — a bulky rad under the window can force a face-fix frame or a slightly shallower panel.
4. **Bay geometry** — splay, box or circular; each section gets its own panels, not one stretched rail.
5. **TV glare and lamp positions** — evening use is as important as the daytime street view.
6. **Colour against walls and sofa** — silk white remains the default; soft greys hide dust better on busy family rooms.

## When Curtains or Blinds May Be Better

We will say so if:

- The reveal is too shallow for louvres to open properly
- You want a heavy thermal drape for a very draughty original sash and are not ready to address the window itself
- A conservatory or fully glazed gable needs specialist blinds rather than shutters

Otherwise [plantation shutters](/our-shutters/) are usually the cleaner long-term finish for a main living room.
