_Last reviewed: August 2026._

Bifold doors are brilliant for opening a kitchen or living room onto the garden — and awkward for window coverings. A single curtain rail across the back wall hides the glass you paid for. A cheap roller across the whole opening catches on handles the first time you fold the doors.

This guide covers **motorised blinds for bifold doors** in plain UK terms: which systems work, how they compare with [BlindScreen®](/blindscreen/) and [shutters](/our-shutters/), realistic 2026 costs, and what we check on a Leicestershire survey.

## The Short Answer

- **Yes — bifolds can take motorised blinds** when each leaf (or a carefully planned group) is measured as its own opening.
- **Per-leaf rollers or honeycombs** are the common motorised solution: blinds travel with the doors.
- **[BlindScreen®](/blindscreen/)** is often better when you need blackout plus a fly screen — usually manual, slim and purpose-built for doors.
- **Tracked shutters** are a different product for a more permanent finish — see our [bi-fold door shutter install](/bi-folding-door-shutters-installation-in-leicestershire/).
- **Expect to pay more than a standard casement** — width, motors and leaf count drive the quote.
- **DIY measuring is risky** on folding doors — stack depth and handle projection matter as much as width and drop.

## How Motorised Blinds Work on Bifolds

A good bifold blind layout does three things at once:

1. Controls glare and privacy across a large glass wall.
2. Lets the doors fold and stack without fabric catching.
3. Keeps operation simple — remote, app or voice — without dangling cords.

![Dark motorised roller blinds on each leaf of black-framed bifold doors in a Loughborough kitchen, controlled via smart home tablet and remote.](/images/blog/motorised-blinds-bifold-doors-hero.jpg)

On most Leicestershire kitchen-diners that means **one cassette per leaf**, aligned with the glazing bars so the run looks intentional when every blind sits at the same drop. Motors are often battery-powered; hard-wired options suit heavier fabrics and wider leaves.

Wide doors cost more than a bedroom casement because you are buying several blinds and several motors — or one specialist door system.

## What We Check on a Bifold Survey

![Hand holding a white remote controlling motorised roller blinds over garden-facing kitchen windows — cord-free operation.](/images/blog/motorised-blinds-bifold-remote-control.jpg)

Mark (or another surveyor) will look at:

1. **Leaf count and stack side** — so blinds park clear when the doors fold.
2. **Handle and hinge projection** — so cassettes and side channels do not foul the furniture.
3. **Power** — battery recharge access vs hard-wired motors.
4. **Fabric job** — light-filtering for living rooms vs blackout for guest bedrooms.

## Common Myths

**“One wide motorised blind will do the whole bifold.”**
It almost never does once the doors open. Per-leaf (or carefully grouped) blinds are the reliable pattern.

**“Motorisation always means an electrician.”**
Battery motors avoid new wiring in many kitchens. Hard-wiring is optional, not automatic.

**“BlindScreen® and motorised rollers are the same thing.”**
They are not. BlindScreen® is a dual-purpose blackout and fly-screen cassette for doors. Motorised rollers are about remote and smart control of everyday fabric.
